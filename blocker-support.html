FAQ

How does blocking work?

On iPhone and iPad, Nuclear Blocker uses Apple’s Screen Time APIs (Family Controls and Managed Settings). After you grant permission, the app applies system-level web content filters to the domains you list. Blocking applies across browsers and apps that load those sites, not inside a single browser extension.

On Mac, blocking uses Apple’s Network Extension content filter. It matches hostnames against your list and drops matching connections system-wide. A local “blocked” page may appear when a supported browser redirects through the companion extension.

Why can’t I cancel a running strict session early?

That is intentional. Strict sessions are designed as a commitment device: once a manual strict session starts, the app will not stop it, shorten it, or let you clear the list until the timer ends. Restarting or force-quitting the app does not end the block. On Mac, quit protection and a watchdog help keep enforcement active during strict mode or an active schedule window.

How do schedules work?

Schedules are recurring weekly windows (weekdays plus start and end times in your local timezone). Overnight windows are supported: if the end time is earlier than the start time, the window crosses midnight. When a schedule is enabled and “now” falls inside its window, blocking turns on with the same strict rules as a manual session. You cannot edit the block list or schedules while blocking is active.

How do iPhone, iPad, and Mac stay in sync?

Devices signed into the same iCloud account share block lists, schedules, and session state through Apple CloudKit in the app’s private iCloud container. Changes pushed from one device merge with the other; an active strict session on either device keeps blocking on until it expires. Open the app or bring it to the foreground to pull the latest state. Background tasks on iOS may also refresh when the system allows.

Sites still load or blocking seems off. What should I check?

  • iOS/iPadOS: Confirm Screen Time permission is granted (Settings → Screen Time, or re-open the app and accept the permission prompt). Make sure the domain is on your list. Force-quit and reopen the app, or wait for a schedule or session to apply.
  • Mac: Confirm the content filter is enabled and you allowed the system extension in System Settings. Check that a strict session or schedule window is actually active. Restart the Mac app if the filter was disabled during installation.
  • Both: Verify iCloud is signed in if you expect cross-device sync. Try adding the site again on the device where you edit the list.

Nuclear Blocker is a personal self-control tool, not MDM-grade device management. Determined users can sometimes find workarounds on any platform.

How do I get a refund for an App Store purchase?

Purchases made through the App Store on iPhone or iPad are handled by Apple. Request a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com. We cannot issue App Store refunds directly.

Where do I download the Mac app?

The companion Mac app is distributed from the Nuclear Blocker product page: modoi.ai/blocker.html (use Get for Mac or the Mac download link). Mac licensing is separate from the iOS/iPadOS App Store build.

Mac license and Paddle

Mac purchases use Paddle hosted checkout in your browser. Paddle is the merchant of record for those transactions. License state is stored on your Mac. If checkout or unlock fails, email support@modoi.ai with your Paddle receipt.
